# CP-56 — Vert.x Web and WebClient shapes, measured

**Version scope.** Everything below is measured against three Vert.x lines, each **as the jars'
own manifests state it** (`Implementation-Version`): **4.5.10**, **5.0.0** and **5.2.1**
(`vertx-web`, `vertx-web-client`, `vertx-web-common`, `vertx-core`, and the RxJava 3 wrapper
`vertx-rx-java3`, which holds `io.vertx.rxjava3.ext.web.*`), plus the two Mutiny wrappers
`smallrye-mutiny-vertx-web` and `-web-client` at **3.23.1** and **4.0.2**. 5.2.1 is the newest 5.2.x
release that is at least two weeks old on the day of the run (5.2.1 is dated 2026-10-07; 5.2.2,
dated 2026-10-21, is newer than the two-week line and is not read). The Mutiny columns carry the
wrapper's own version; the Vert.x release each wraps is the `vertx.version` of its parent pom
(`smallrye-mutiny-vertx-bindings-projects`): **3.23.1 wraps 4.5.34, 4.0.2 wraps 5.1.8**. That
mapping is read from the pom, not from the jar. `probe.py` reads each jar out of the local Maven
repository and takes the version from the jar, never from the directory it found it under (the CP-5
failure the convention exists to prevent), and aborts when they disagree. The probe module is
compiled by javac 21 (class file major version 65, checked) against Vert.x **5.0.0**, the version
the fixture builds with. These are shapes of those releases; 4.5.11 to 4.5.35, 5.0.1 to 5.1.x and a
later 5.2.x are free to change any of them, so every verdict is bound to the columns it is read
from and to nothing wider.

**Why it was measured.** The Vert.x Web design (spec 2026-10-30, §2a, §3) gives two new catalogs
(`vertx-web.yml`, `vertx-webclient.yml`) and one client model their whole framework knowledge, and
adds a `returns_receiver` flag to every self-returning builder step. Each rests on a fact about
Vert.x's own bytecode: which methods exist and with what descriptor, which of them answer their
receiver, what a route with no verb or no path matches, what Vert.x refuses to register, where a
`WebClient` takes its default host and port from, how many constants `HttpMethod` declares, what the
RxJava 3 and Mutiny wrappers call the same methods, and what javac writes for the call forms the
catalogs read. This record is what each new catalog cites in its `measured:` block, so the guesses
are replaced by readings before any catalog states them.

**What this probe does not settle.** It reads bytecode; it runs no Vert.x code. Three of its
readings are structural and say so:
- "answers its own receiver" is read as: every `areturn` of the method is `aload_0` (optionally
  through a `checkcast`) or the result of a call to a method of the same class chain that itself
  answers its receiver (or to an abstract method returning the same type). The "result of a call"
  reading does not check that the call's receiver is `this`; for the classes read here every such
  call is on `this`;
- the default-host/port rule (e) is read from which `getDefaultHost`/`getDefaultPort` calls each
  method makes and from the order of the stores at send time, not by running a request;
- the guards of the refusals (d) are the calls and constants read since the previous `athrow`, not
  a decompilation.
Not measured: a Mutiny or RxJava 3 wrapper of `WebClientConfig` (5.2.1 introduces it, §(a)); the
Kotlin coroutine and `RouterBuilder` (OpenAPI) shapes; Vert.x 3.x; `WebClientSession`,
`CachingWebClient`, `OAuth2WebClient`.

**How to run.** `JAVA_HOME=<jdk 21> bash scripts/probes/cp56_vertx_web/run.sh`. It fetches any jar
above that is not yet in `~/.m2` (offline first, network only for what is missing), compiles
`scripts/probes/cp56_vertx_web/module/` with Maven after a `clean` (so no cell can come from a stale
build), then runs `probe.py`, which prints the tables below. Not a test: nothing in the suite runs
it.

## What the rows say

**(a) Rows 1–100 — every name §2a lists exists, with these exceptions.** Rows 1–22 (`Router`),
23–41 (`Route`), 42–57 (`WebClient`), 58–79 (`HttpRequest`), 80–84 (`WebClientOptions`), 85–89
(`WebClientConfig`) and 90–100 (`RequestOptions`). Every `Router` name, including all nine verbs
with `x()`, `x(String)` and `xWithRegex(String)`, and every `Route` name has the **same descriptor
in 4.5.10, 5.0.0 and 5.2.1**, except:
- `Router.mountSubRouter(String, Router)` exists in **4.5.10 only** (row 4);
- `HttpRequest.expect(ResponsePredicate)` and `expect(Function)` exist in **4.5.10 only** (row 71).
  §2a lists `expect` among the 24 self-returning methods; at 5.0.0 and 5.2.1 there is none;
- `WebClient.request` gains `request(RequestOptions)` at 5.2.1 (row 56), and `WebClient.create` gains
  four overloads taking `WebClientConfig` (row 42);
- `HttpRequest.send…` in 4.5.10 has each terminal twice, `()Future` and `(Handler)V`; 5.0.0 and
  5.2.1 have the `Future` form only (rows 73–79), as §2a says. 5.2.1 adds
  `putHeader(CharSequence, CharSequence)` and `putHeader(CharSequence, Iterable)` (row 63);
- `RequestOptions.setServer` takes `SocketAddress` in 4.5.10 and `Address` in 5.0.0 and 5.2.1 (row
  95). `getHost`, `getPort` and `isSsl` are declared by `RequestOptions` in 4.5.10 and **inherited
  from `HttpConnectOptions`** in 5.x (rows 97, 98, 100); `WebClientOptions.getDefaultHost` and
  `getDefaultPort` are inherited from `HttpClientOptions` in all three (rows 83, 84);
- **5.2.1 introduces `io.vertx.ext.web.client.WebClientConfig`** (rows 85–89, 104), a second
  configuration type with its own `setDefaultHost(String)`, `setDefaultPort(int)` and `setSsl(boolean)`
  (each answering `WebClientConfig`), and `WebClient.create(Vertx, WebClientConfig[, PoolOptions |
  ClientSSLOptions])` beside the `WebClientOptions` overloads, which are still there (row 42). A
  catalog that flags only `WebClientOptions`'s setters misses a 5.2 client built from a
  `WebClientConfig`. `WebClientOptions` itself still extends `HttpClientOptions` (rows 103, 112).

Every spec'd `WebClient` verb form exists in all three: `x(String)`, `x(String, String)`,
`x(int, String, String)`, each again with `UriTemplate`, and `xAbs(String)`/`xAbs(UriTemplate)`
(rows 43–57); `request(HttpMethod, …)` has the `SocketAddress` and `RequestOptions` forms too
(row 56).

`router.get(…).handler(…)`: the static owner is the **interface** `io.vertx.ext.web.Route`, not
`RouteImpl`, and for the client it is `io.vertx.ext.web.client.HttpRequest`. So the flag list is
the interface's, rows 110–111, and the implementation rows 101–102 cross-check it.
- **`Route`** (row 110): 4.5.10 **22** (20 abstract + `respond(Function)` and
  `useNormalisedPath(boolean)`, both defaults answering a self-returning call); 5.0.0 and 5.2.1 **21**
  (20 + `respond`). `RouteImpl` answers `this` for every one of the 20 abstract methods ("does not
  answer `this` for: none"). `RouteImpl` itself (row 101) has 18 methods that return `this` directly
  and 3 (`blockingHandler` ×2, `last()`) that return the result of a call to one that does; its
  only name `Route` lacks is `setEmptyBodyPermittedWithConsumes(boolean)`.
- **`HttpRequest`** (row 111): 25 abstract methods return `HttpRequest`; **24 answer `this`
  in `HttpRequestImpl` and one, `copy()`, does not** (it builds a new request). Plus defaults that
  answer a self-returning call: 4.5.10 **4** (`basicAuthentication` ×2, `bearerTokenAuthentication`,
  `expect(Function)`), 5.0.0 **3**, 5.2.1 **5** (those three and the two `putHeader(CharSequence,
  …)`). So the owner `HttpRequest` has **28 / 27 / 29** self-returning methods at 4.5.10 / 5.0.0 /
  5.2.1. `HttpRequestImpl` itself (row 102) answers `this` directly in **24 / 24 / 26**; the three
  defaults (`basicAuthentication`, `bearerTokenAuthentication`) are **not** among the Impl's — §2a's

  owners.
- **`WebClientOptions`** (row 103): only 4 methods return `this` literally (`setFollowRedirects`,
  `setTemplateExpandOptions`, `setUserAgent`, `setUserAgentEnabled`). The other **84 / 60 / 60** are

**(c) Rows 113–117 — an empty method set matches any method, a null path matches any path.**
`RouteState.matches` is identical in all three versions. The path test runs only when
`path != null && pattern == null` (row 113); a route with neither a path nor a pattern goes straight
on to the method test, so it matches **any path**. The method test is
`methods` empty (the helper treats null as empty too, row 115) skips `containsMethod`, so an empty
set matches **any method**; a miss answers 405 (row 114). `Router.route()` builds
`new RouteImpl(router, order)`, whose constructor stores neither a path nor a method (row 117), so
`route()` is a route for every path and every method; `route(String)` and `route(HttpMethod, String)`
call `checkPath` then `setPath`, and the regex forms call `setRegex`.

**(d) Rows 118–122 — what Vert.x refuses to register.**
- `RouteImpl.checkPath` throws `IllegalArgumentException("Path must start with /")` when the
  path equals `""` or its first character is not `/` (row 118). It is called from `path(String)` and
  from the two constructors that take a plain path, i.e. `route(String)`, `route(HttpMethod, String)`,
  `get(String)` and the other verbs, and `.path(String)` (row 121). It is **not** called from
  the regex constructors (`routeWithRegex`, `pathRegex`). Identical in all three.
- `RouteImpl.subRouter` throws `IllegalStateException` for a route that already has a path that
  `RouteState.isExactPath` reports as exact ("Sub router cannot be mounted on an exact path."), for a
  route with no path but a pattern ("…on a regular expression path."), and for a second
  `subRouter` on the same route ("Only one sub router per Route object is allowed.") (row 119).
  A route with no path and no pattern (`route().subRouter(r)`) passes all three tests. Identical in
  all three. This probe records the calls (`getPath`, `isExactPath`, `getPattern`, `isExclusive`)
  guarding each message; that a path ending in `*` is "not exact" is §2a's reading, not repeated
  here.
- 4.5.10's `RouterImpl.mountSubRouter(String, Router)` throws
  `IllegalArgumentException("Don't include * when mounting a sub router")` after
  `mountPoint.endsWith("*")` (row 120), then builds the mount path with a `StringBuilder` and calls
  `route(…)` and `Route.subRouter` (row 122). It is absent at 5.0.0 and 5.2.1.

**(e) Rows 123–132 — where the default host and port apply, and when the request's own wins.**
The only class of `vertx-web-client` that calls `getDefaultHost` or `getDefaultPort` is
**`WebClientBase`** (row 123); `HttpRequestImpl` never reads them. `WebClientBase.options` is
`WebClientOptions` at 4.5.10 and 5.0.0 and **`WebClientConfig`** at 5.2.1 (row 125). The default is
applied **when the request object is built**, by the `WebClientBase.request…` overload the call
reaches (rows 124, 127); every `get`/`post`/`put`/`delete`/`patch`/`head` is the same default method
with the verb swapped (row 127: each form is "[6 method(s)]"), reaching:

| written | default host | default port | what the request starts with |
|---|---|---|---|
| `x(uri)`, `x(UriTemplate)`, `request(m, uri)` | **applied** | **applied** | host = default host, port = default port, uri as written |
| `x(host, uri)`, `request(m, host, uri)` | the call's own | **applied** | host as written, port = default port |
| `x(port, host, uri)` | the call's own | the call's own | both as written; the defaults are not read |
| `request(m, RequestOptions)` | applied **only if** `getHost()` is null | applied **only if** `getPort()` is null | each part separately: kept when non-null, else the default (row 126; at 5.2.1 the test lives in `request(RequestOptions)`, which the `HttpMethod` form calls) |
| `xAbs(url)`, `requestAbs(m, url)` | not read | not read | host, port, ssl and uri **parsed from the URL** by `ClientUri.parse`; the method holds the integer constants 80 and 443 and the string `ftp` (row 131) for a URL without a port |
| `xAbs(UriTemplate)` | not read | not read | `host = null`, `port = -1`, the template stored as `absoluteUri` (row 128); expanded and parsed at send time |

Then, at **send time**, `HttpRequestImpl.buildRequestOptions` applies, in this order, the request's
own `ssl` if non-null, `port` **if >= 0**, `host` **if non-null**, over what the absolute URL parsed
into (row 130). The setters `host(String)`, `port(int)`, `ssl(Boolean)` store into the same fields
(row 129). So: **the default fills only the host and port a call did not name; a request's own
`.host(…)` / `.port(…)` replaces whatever was there, the default and an absolute URL's alike;
an absolute URL beats the default, because `requestAbs` never reads it.** A `WebClientOptions` or
`WebClientConfig` with no default set holds `localhost` and `80` (row 132; at 5.2.1 `HttpClientConfig()`
stores them in the constructor). The relative-address join is therefore the host/port above, not a
URL join: the request's uri is a path-and-query string sent to that host and port.

**Where this record differs from the spec** (flagged for the catalogs and the plan):
1. **`HttpMethod` has 31 constants at 4.5.10 and 5.0.0 and 32 at 5.2.1** (adds `QUERY`); §2a says 31.
2. **`HttpRequest.expect` is 4.5.10 only**; §2a lists it among the methods that return `this`.
3. **"24 methods return `this`" is `HttpRequestImpl` only.** The owner a caller names is the
   `HttpRequest` interface, whose self-returning set is 28 / 27 / 29 (24 abstract the Impl answers,
   plus 4 / 3 / 5 defaults); `basicAuthentication` and `bearerTokenAuthentication` are defaults on the
   interface, not Impl methods.
4. **`WebClientOptions`' setters return `this` through `super`**, 84 / 60 / 60 of them, plus
   11 / 12 / 18 inherited setters that answer `this` with the superclass's declared type.
5. **5.2.1 adds `WebClientConfig`** and `WebClient.create(Vertx, WebClientConfig…)`; the
   defaults are read from it.
6. **`Router.mountSubRouter` and `HttpRequest.expect` are 4.5-only**; `RequestOptions.setServer`
   changes parameter type between 4.5.10 and 5.0.0.
7. §2a's "a request naming its own host or port keeps it" is exact for `x(host, uri)`,
   `x(port, host, uri)` and `request(m, RequestOptions)`, and is also true after `.host(…)` /
   `.port(…)`; the **default is read at the call that builds the request**, not at send time.
8. Mutiny's `Route.blockingHandler` is `(Handler, boolean)` at 3.23.1 and `(Consumer, boolean)` at
   4.0.2; its `handler`/`failureHandler` take `Consumer` at both.
