# CP-37 — how large the operand product actually gets

Spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-18-partly-known-addresses-design.md` §4, which declares
**no cap** on the product a multi-hole join enumerates and says the decision waits on a
measurement over every corpus **plus Apollo**. This is that measurement.

Re-run it rather than trusting this file:
`.venv/bin/python scripts/probes/cp37_join_product/product_sizes.py`. It counts by replacing
`values.product` and wrapping `values._Walk._joined`, so every number is the shipped code's
own rather than a re-implementation of it.

**Version scope.** Everything below is measured against **Sextant 0.1.0** — the version the
run read out of the installed distribution's own metadata and printed before measuring
anything. What is measured here is Sextant's own join arm rather than a third-party library,
so that distribution IS the configuration, and a later one is free to change the arm and
therefore the table. The corpora are pinned: 18 built fixtures, the five field pins, and
`apolloconfig/apollo` at `ea35677`.

## What the question is, exactly

`_joined` builds one candidate list per hole and enumerates the product of those lists. The
risk §4 names is the obvious one: a hole with many candidates, times another hole with many
candidates, is a combinatorial blow-up in a function nothing rate-limits.

It is sharpened by a figure measured the same day. `apollo-portal` reaches **685 paths** at
one position (`PortalMetaDomainService#getMetaServerAddress`, depth 18, 3,365 visits), against
an estate-wide maximum of **11** across the previous 27 corpora. If paths became candidates,
that one position would be the blow-up.

## Why paths did not become candidates

**A path is not a candidate.** A hole contributes one candidate per path on which it reached
a `string` LITERAL; a hole that reached none contributes exactly one — itself. Apollo's
685-path position is `getMetaServerAddress`, which resolves to no string at all, so it
contributes one. The 64-caller position (`doExecute`'s `path` parameter) resolves to no string
either, for the reason `OI-143` now records: the local is reassigned a frame above the call
and the fact is a bare `{kind: unresolved}` with no `source`.

So §4's stated reason holds on the data: the product is over HOLES, and the hole count is the
recipe's own — one or two everywhere in this estate.

## The verdict, and what would change it

**No cap**, and the number behind that is 2. What would change it is a corpus where one hole
resolves many ways AND another resolves many ways on coherent paths — two callers' worth of
literals in both halves of one assembled address. Nothing in 31 corpora does. Re-run this
probe when one appears; the figure to watch is `combinations, max`, and the ceiling worth
caring about is where it approaches the path counts beside it (685 here, 2 observed).
